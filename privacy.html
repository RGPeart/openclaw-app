OpenClaw Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 6, 2026

OpenClaw ("the app") is a personal assistant run by Ryan Peart ("the owner") on his own private server, for his own use. This policy explains what Google user data the app accesses and how it is handled.

Who this applies to

The app has a single user: the owner. It is not offered to the public and no one else signs in to it.

Google data the app accesses

When the owner signs in with Google and grants permission, the app can access the following in the owner's own Google account:

  • Gmail: read and search messages, and create or send messages. Messages are sent only after the owner has approved the exact content.
  • Google Calendar: read and create events.
  • Google Drive, Docs, Sheets and Slides: read, create and edit files.

How the data is used

Data is used only to carry out tasks the owner asks for, such as summarising mail, drafting a reply, checking the calendar, or preparing a document. It is not used for advertising, profiling or any other purpose.

Storage and sharing

  • Google data is processed on the owner's private server.
  • To complete a task the owner requested, excerpts of Google data may be sent to AI model providers (currently Anthropic, with Google and NVIDIA as fallback providers). Those providers handle the data under their own terms.
  • Google data is not sold, rented, or shared with anyone else for their own use, except as needed to provide the features the owner requests, for security or legal reasons, or with the owner's consent.
  • The app does not give anyone other than the owner access to the data, except where required by law or to the extent an AI model provider's own terms allow.

Google API Services User Data Policy

The app's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. The app itself does not use Google data to develop, improve or train AI or machine learning models.

What is stored and for how long

  • Access tokens are stored on the owner's server until access is revoked or the owner deletes them.
  • Task records. Conversation logs and working notes from tasks may contain excerpts of Google data (for example part of an email or a calendar entry). They are kept on the owner's server until the owner deletes them.

Revoking access and deletion

The owner can revoke the app's access at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions. Revoking access makes the stored tokens useless, and the owner can delete the tokens and any stored task records from his server.

Changes

If this policy changes, the updated version will be posted here with a new date.
